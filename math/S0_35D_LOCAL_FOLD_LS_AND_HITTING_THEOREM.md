# S0 local 35-parameter fold theorem — proof draft for independent scrutiny

Date: 2026-11-02. **Scope: mathematical proof only, no simulations or G1.** The result is local and standard in its methods; novelty is not claimed.

## Statement
Fix a sufficiently small Frobenius radius delta>0 with closed ball B(T*,2delta) contained in the LS-valid open set U. For each T_init in B(T*,delta) with a(T_init)<0, and each h>0, select T_cross in B(T*,2delta) with a(T_cross)>0; then select a bounded connected U0 with closure(U0) subset B(T*,2delta) containing T_init and T_cross and an interior continuous path gamma:[0,h]->U0 connecting them. For example, after choosing delta so that the closed ball of radius 2delta is contained in U, take U0=B(T*,3delta/2), whose closure lies in B(T*,2delta). For each T_init in B(T*,delta) on the a<0 side, choose a fixed T_cross in B(T*,delta/2) on the a>0 side; the straight segment joining them lies inside U0. Such a T_cross exists because Da(T*)[E_mu]=1. The probability conclusion is qualitative and depends on T_init,h,U0,sigma; no explicit lower bound is claimed.

Let V=Sym^4(R^4) with Frobenius metric and T*=T_{mu=0} given by quartic f=x1^4+2x1²x2²+x1*x2³. In a neighborhood U of T*, there is a smooth function a:U->R with a(T*)=0, Da(T*) nonzero, and a smooth critical-point location t_c(T), such that: (i) the local Z-eigenpairs near [e1] are exactly two simple projective branches for a(T)<0, one corank-one degenerate local critical point for a(T)=0, and none for a(T)>0; (ii) the local discriminant Sigma={T in U:a(T)=0} is a smooth embedded codimension-one hypersurface; (iii) for every sufficiently nearby T_init in U with a(T_init)<0, and any prescribed horizon h>0, choose a bounded connected neighborhood U_init with closure contained in U and a continuous path gamma:[0,h]->U_init from T_init to T_cross with a(T_cross)>0 and positive distance from U_init's complement; there is a continuous branch X_± on {T in U_init:a(T)<0}, such that the isotropic tensor Brownian path T_s=T_init+sigma B_s, sigma>0, has strictly positive probability to reach Sigma before time h and before leaving U_init. Along that event both X_+(T_s) and X_-(T_s) converge to the same local eigenvector at the hitting time and its tangent Hessian becomes singular. This is *local first degeneracy of the selected branch*, not global branch death or first singularity among all tensor eigenpairs.

## 1. Chart and transverse elimination
Choose projective sign x1>0 and chart y=(t,u,v), x(y)=(1,t,u,v)/sqrt(1+t²+u²+v²). Define the smooth rational g(y,T)=T[(1,t,u,v)^4]/(1+t²+u²+v²)^2. Z-eigenpairs in this chart are exactly critical points of g. For T*, y=0, grad_y g=0. Its chart Hessian is diag(0,-4,-4), equal to four times tangent H at e1. More generally at a critical point, writing J(y)=D_y x(y) (a 4-by-3 full-rank chart Jacobian), one has D_y²g(y,T)=4 J(y)^T H(T,x(y)) J(y). This follows from the constrained critical equation T x³=lambda x and x·partial_ij x=-partial_i x·partial_j x. Thus chart Hessian and tangent H have identical rank and corank, although they are not literally the same matrix away from the origin. In particular, the transverse block D²_{(u,v)}g=-4 I2 is invertible. By continuity it stays invertible on a small neighborhood. The implicit function theorem applied to (g_u,g_v)=0 gives a unique smooth h(t,T)=(u(t,T),v(t,T)), h(0,T*)=0, for all sufficiently nearby (t,T). For the restricted T_mu family, h(t,T_mu)=0 exactly near the origin (since the numerator is independent of u,v and g_u=-4u*N/(1+|y|²)^3, analogously for v, with N near 1).

Define q(t,T)=g_t(t,h(t,T),T). Critical points of g near y=0 are equivalent to q(t,T)=0 and (u,v)=h(t,T). Because g_{t,(u,v)}(0,T*)=0, h_t(0,T*)=0. At the base, q(t,T_mu)= [mu(1-3t²)+3t²-4t³-t⁴]/(1+t²)^3. Thus q(0,T*)=q_t(0,T*)=0, q_tt(0,T*)=6>0, and D_T q(0,T*)[E_mu]=1 for E_mu with quartic x1³x2 (Frobenius norm 1/2). These derivatives are evaluated for the FULL 35-parameter smooth function q, not by assuming other coefficients vanish.

## 2. Discriminant sheet and branch availability
Since q_tt(0,T*)=6, the implicit function theorem for q_t(t,T)=0 gives unique smooth t_c(T) with t_c(T*)=0. Define a(T)=q(t_c(T),T). Then Da(T*)[E_mu]=D_Tq(0,T*)[E_mu]+q_t(0,T*) Dt_c[E_mu]=1, hence Da(T*) !=0. The level set Sigma=a^{-1}(0) is a smooth codimension-one sheet after shrinking U.

Shrink neighborhoods so q_tt(t,T)>c>0 uniformly. Then q(t,T) is strictly convex in t near t_c(T). By the parameterized Morse lemma, or elementary Taylor with uniform positive quadratic remainder, q(t,T)=a(T)+(t-t_c(T))² b(t,T), with smooth b>0. After restricting U and the t-window, for a(T)<0 sufficiently small there are exactly two simple zeros t_-(T)<t_c(T)<t_+(T), for a(T)=0 one double zero, for a(T)>0 none. The roots are smooth on the open side a<0, and extend continuously to t_c at a=0. This counts only roots inside a fixed sufficiently small t-window and the associated transverse chart neighborhood, not all eigenpairs of T. For the restricted family q_tt(0,T_mu)=6-12mu. Thus mu<1/2 is the exact positivity condition at t=0 along this restricted line; the symmetric sufficient restriction |mu|<1/2 gives positivity there but not by itself a uniform bound for nonzero t or other tensor directions. For example |mu|<=1/4 gives q_tt(0,T_mu)>=3. By continuity of the full smooth q_tt(t,T), choose a sufficiently small t-window and 35-dimensional Frobenius neighborhood of T* to ensure q_tt(t,T)>=c>0 throughout. The original proof uses only q_tt(0,T*)=6; this is a local neighborhood argument, not a claim about the whole restricted family. A small enough joint neighborhood ensures both roots remain in the chart and no other local critical points occur. The transverse Hessian block M=g_{(u,v),(u,v)} is invertible. Write the full chart Hessian in (t,(u,v)) blocks as [[A,B],[B^T,M]], where A=g_tt and B=g_{t,(u,v)}. Differentiating g_{(u,v)}(t,h(t,T),T)=0 gives h_t=-M^{-1}B^T, hence q_t=A+B h_t=A-BM^{-1}B^T, exactly the Schur complement. Therefore rank(D_y²g)=rank(M)+rank(q_t). At either simple root q_t !=0, so the full Hessian and H are invertible; at a=0, q_t=0 while M is invertible, so both have corank exactly one. Thus the corresponding Z-eigenpairs are regular precisely on the two-root side and corank one at the fold.

## 3. Adapted branch selection and stopping
For a bounded open tensor-parameter neighborhood U0 with closure(U0) contained in the LS-valid set U and with the local root window valid throughout, define X_±(T)=x(t_±(T),h(t_±(T),T)) for T in U0 with a(T)<0. These are deterministic continuous (indeed smooth) functions of T on their domain, hence X_±(T_s) are adapted continuous for continuous adapted T_s. Let tau_U=inf{s>=0:T_s notin U0}, define the closed compact set K= {T in closure(U0):a(T)=0} (well-defined since closure(U0) subset U), let tau_K=inf{s>=0:T_s in K} (inf empty set=infinity), and rho=tau_U wedge tau_K. These are stopping times for continuous adapted T_s because K is closed. On the strict event tau_K<tau_U, hitting K is exactly hitting the local sheet while still inside U0. Boundary hits at tau_K=tau_U are classified as chart exit, not interior fold access. No evaluation of a outside U is required. On s<rho, selected X_± are defined, adapted and regular; at tau_K<tau_U, both X_+ and X_- have the same continuous one-sided limit x(t_c(T_{tau_K}),h(t_c(T_{tau_K}),T_{tau_K})) and H has a zero eigenvalue. We make no claim that the branch can be continued as a regular branch beyond that time or that no other eigenvectors exist.

## 4. Positive-probability local hitting by full 35D Brownian motion
Let sigma>0 and h>0. Choose a bounded open U0 with closure(U0) contained in U and a nearby T_init in U0 with a(T_init)<0. **Assume explicitly** that a continuous gamma:[0,h]->U0 exists with gamma(0)=T_init, a(gamma(h))=eta>0 and d=dist(gamma([0,h]),U0^c)>0. For the theorem's ordered choice, first fix delta and T_init in B(T*,delta) with a(T_init)<0, then fix h>0, choose U0=B(T*,3delta/2), and choose T_cross in B(T*,delta/2) with a(T_cross)>0; finally take gamma(s)=(1-s/h)T_init+(s/h)T_cross. The segment lies inside the convex ball U0, and compactness of its image gives d>0. The sign-changing endpoint is guaranteed by Da(T*)[E_mu]=1 and the choice of sufficiently small delta. By uniform continuity of a on a compact neighborhood of gamma([0,h]), choose epsilon in (0,d) so that any path epsilon-close to gamma has a(T_h)>eta/2 and remains in U0. Since the starting tensor is fixed with a(T_init)<0, every such path crosses a=0 by continuity. Nondegenerate 35D Brownian motion has full support in C_0([0,h];V) in the uniform topology, so P(sup_{s<=h}||T_s-gamma(s)||<epsilon)>0. This is strict positivity, not an explicit numerical lower bound; the positive probability depends on the initial tensor, time horizon, domain and sigma, and no uniform estimate as h tends to zero or the domain shrinks is asserted; it follows from full support of the nondegenerate Brownian law on continuous paths starting at T_init. Thus P(tau_K<tau_U, tau_K<=h)>0. This proves a local hitting event for full isotropic tensor BM, not a hitting-time density or an almost-sure/global theorem. The nonzero normal diffusion is also explicit: Da(T*)[E_mu]=1 and the Frobenius-unit direction E_1112=2E_mu yields Da(T*)[E_1112]=2, so sigma²||Da(T*)||_F² >=4sigma²>0.

## 5. Scope, limitations, and remaining audits
The theorem is for starts sufficiently close to T* on the a<0 side in a specified ball admitting an interior sign-changing path, not arbitrary tensors or arbitrary disconnected neighborhoods. It concerns a locally selected fold branch, not an intrinsic globally maximally continued eigenvector. No global first-singularity distribution, global branch-death theorem, or critical exponent is proved. A later independent mathematical audit should scrutinize the uniform root-window argument, the precise neighborhood quantifiers for every T_init, and the path-support argument. The proof uses standard implicit-function, parameterized fold, and Brownian support principles; it is not a confirmed new discovery. S0 remains pending independent acceptance, G1 and experiments blocked.
