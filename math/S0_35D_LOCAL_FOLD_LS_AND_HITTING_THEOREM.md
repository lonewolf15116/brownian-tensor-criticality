# S0 local 35-parameter fold theorem — proof draft for independent scrutiny

Date: 2026-11-02. **Scope: mathematical proof only, no simulations or G1.** The result is local and standard in its methods; novelty is not claimed.

## Statement
Let V=Sym^4(R^4) with Frobenius metric and T*=T_{mu=0} given by quartic f=x1^4+2x1²x2²+x1*x2³. In a neighborhood U of T*, there is a smooth function a:U->R with a(T*)=0, Da(T*) nonzero, and a smooth critical-point location t_c(T), such that: (i) the local Z-eigenpairs near [e1] are exactly two simple projective branches for a(T)<0, one degenerate pair for a(T)=0, and none for a(T)>0; (ii) the local discriminant Sigma={T in U:a(T)=0} is a smooth embedded codimension-one hypersurface; (iii) for every sufficiently nearby T_init in U with a(T_init)<0, and any prescribed horizon h>0, there exists a smaller bounded neighborhood U_init subset U containing T_init and a continuous branch X_± on {T in U_init:a(T)<0}, such that the isotropic tensor Brownian path T_s=T_init+sigma B_s, sigma>0, has strictly positive probability to reach Sigma before time h and before leaving U_init. Along that event X_±(T_s) has a continuous limit at the hitting time and its tangent Hessian becomes singular. This is *local first degeneracy of the selected branch*, not global branch death or first singularity among all tensor eigenpairs.

## 1. Chart and transverse elimination
Choose projective sign x1>0 and chart y=(t,u,v), x(y)=(1,t,u,v)/sqrt(1+t²+u²+v²). Define the smooth rational g(y,T)=T[(1,t,u,v)^4]/(1+t²+u²+v²)^2. Z-eigenpairs in this chart are exactly critical points of g. For T*, y=0, grad_y g=0. Its chart Hessian is diag(0,-4,-4), equal to four times tangent H at e1. In particular, the transverse block D²_{(u,v)}g=-4 I2 is invertible. By continuity it stays invertible on a small neighborhood. The implicit function theorem applied to (g_u,g_v)=0 gives a unique smooth h(t,T)=(u(t,T),v(t,T)), h(0,T*)=0, for all sufficiently nearby (t,T). For the restricted T_mu family, h(t,T_mu)=0 exactly near the origin (since the numerator is independent of u,v and g_u=-4u*N/(1+|y|²)^3, analogously for v, with N near 1).

Define q(t,T)=g_t(t,h(t,T),T). Critical points of g near y=0 are equivalent to q(t,T)=0 and (u,v)=h(t,T). Because g_{t,(u,v)}(0,T*)=0, h_t(0,T*)=0. At the base, q(t,T_mu)= [mu(1-3t²)+3t²-4t³-t⁴]/(1+t²)^3. Thus q(0,T*)=q_t(0,T*)=0, q_tt(0,T*)=6>0, and D_T q(0,T*)[E_mu]=1 for E_mu with quartic x1³x2 (Frobenius norm 1/2). These derivatives are evaluated for the FULL 35-parameter smooth function q, not by assuming other coefficients vanish.

## 2. Discriminant sheet and branch availability
Since q_tt(0,T*)=6, the implicit function theorem for q_t(t,T)=0 gives unique smooth t_c(T) with t_c(T*)=0. Define a(T)=q(t_c(T),T). Then Da(T*)[E_mu]=D_Tq(0,T*)[E_mu]+q_t(0,T*) Dt_c[E_mu]=1, hence Da(T*) !=0. The level set Sigma=a^{-1}(0) is a smooth codimension-one sheet after shrinking U.

Shrink neighborhoods so q_tt(t,T)>c>0 uniformly. Then q(t,T) is strictly convex in t near t_c(T). By the parameterized Morse lemma, or elementary Taylor with uniform positive quadratic remainder, q(t,T)=a(T)+(t-t_c(T))² b(t,T), with smooth b>0. After restricting U and the t-window, for a(T)<0 sufficiently small there are exactly two simple zeros t_-(T)<t_c(T)<t_+(T), for a(T)=0 one double zero, for a(T)>0 none. The roots are smooth on the open side a<0, and extend continuously to t_c at a=0. A small enough joint neighborhood ensures both roots remain in the chart and no other local critical points occur. The transverse Hessian block is invertible; by its Schur complement, the full chart Hessian is invertible at a simple root (q_t !=0) and singular at a=0 (q_t=0). Thus the corresponding Z-eigenpairs are regular precisely on the two-root side and corank one at the fold.

## 3. Adapted branch selection and stopping
For a bounded relatively compact chart neighborhood U0 subset U on which the two roots exist for a<0, define X_±(T)=x(t_±(T),h(t_±(T),T)) for T in U0 with a(T)<0. These are deterministic continuous (indeed smooth) functions of T on their domain, hence X_±(T_s) are adapted continuous for continuous adapted T_s. Let tau_U=inf{s:T_s notin U0}, tau_Sigma=inf{s:a(T_s)=0}, and rho=tau_U wedge tau_Sigma. They are stopping times (a continuous on U0; extend or localize as needed). On s<rho, selected X_± remain regular; at tau_Sigma<tau_U, X_± has continuous limit at t_c(T_tau) and H has a zero eigenvalue. We make no claim that the branch can be continued as a regular branch beyond that time or that no other eigenvectors exist.

## 4. Positive-probability local hitting by full 35D Brownian motion
Let sigma>0. For a nearby T_init with a(T_init)<0, choose T_cross in U0 with a(T_cross)>0 and a deterministic continuous path gamma:[0,h]->U0 from T_init to T_cross (e.g. a short line segment along E_mu near the base fold). Shrink U0 or choose the path so its image is a positive distance from boundary. Since a(gamma(0))<0 and a(gamma(h))>0, any sufficiently close continuous path in uniform norm crosses a=0 by the intermediate value theorem while remaining in U0. Nondegenerate 35D Brownian motion has full support in C_0([0,h];V) in the uniform topology, so P(sup_{s<=h}||T_s-gamma(s)||<epsilon)>0. Thus P(tau_Sigma<tau_U, tau_Sigma<=h)>0. This proves a local hitting event for full isotropic tensor BM, not a hitting-time density or an almost-sure/global theorem. The nonzero normal diffusion is also explicit: Da(T*)[E_mu]=1 and the Frobenius-unit direction E_1112=2E_mu yields Da(T*)[E_1112]=2, so sigma²||Da(T*)||_F² >=4sigma²>0.

## 5. Scope, limitations, and remaining audits
The theorem is for starts sufficiently close to T* on the a<0 side, not arbitrary tensors. It concerns a locally selected fold branch, not an intrinsic globally maximally continued eigenvector. No global first-singularity distribution, global branch-death theorem, or critical exponent is proved. A later independent mathematical audit should scrutinize the uniform root-window argument, the precise neighborhood quantifiers for every T_init, and the path-support argument. The proof uses standard implicit-function, parameterized fold, and Brownian support principles; it is not a confirmed new discovery. S0 remains pending independent acceptance, G1 and experiments blocked.
